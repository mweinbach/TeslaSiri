# Morph paths and foreground content

Liquid Glass morphs the **material outline**. Symbols, labels, hit regions, and accessibility meaning remain the control's foreground responsibilities. Apple shows two symbols inside a blended glass shape; the material does not turn those symbols into one icon. See [Applying Liquid Glass to custom views](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/applying-liquid-glass-to-custom-views) and [`glassEffect(_:in:)`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/glasseffect(_:in:)).

## Choose the effect path

| Desired behavior | Use | Foreground implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A Commander guest peels from or returns to a host while retaining control identity and interruptibility | Keep both effects mounted in one `GlassEffectContainer`; animate the guest's frame/offset and keep IDs stable. | Animate icon/label visibility separately. The guest can be a visually tiny seed, but its inactive action must leave hit testing and accessibility. |
| A control truly appears or disappears in the hierarchy | Give each effect a unique `glassEffectID` in one namespace and animate insertion/removal. Use the container's default `.matchedGeometry` transition for nearby effects; choose `.materialize` for a farther effect or a simpler transition. | The transition can animate its own content. Avoid adding a second manual fade before inspecting intermediate frames. |
| Several contributions must remain one material shape at rest | Use `glassEffectUnion` with a shared union ID and similar shape/effect. | Each contribution still owns its content and semantics. Changing union IDs is not a frame interpolator. |

Apple's [`GlassEffectTransition`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/glasseffecttransition) defines insertion/removal effects. [`matchedGeometry`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/glasseffecttransition/matchedgeometry) derives an entering shape from a nearby glass effect within container spacing; [`materialize`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/glasseffecttransition/materialize) animates material and fades content without geometry matching. This path is different from Commander's mounted-guest geometry motion. A stable ID preserves effect correspondence; changing an ID does not cause two resting outlines to fuse.

Choose container `spacing` against the real gap between effects. Increasing it makes the material join earlier and can merge separate controls at rest. Apply `.glassEffect` after modifiers that affect the view's appearance so the container captures the intended content. Apple's examples use `40` points to demonstrate the mechanism, not as a Commander metric. [Apple's custom-view guide](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/applying-liquid-glass-to-custom-views) explains spacing and modifier placement.

## Compact trigger to menu

A reviewed screen recording shows a small titlebar glass capsule expanding into a large rounded menu. Its outline stretches through a short connected neck while the trigger glyphs soften and disappear; menu icons and labels are briefly optically soft before becoming sharp as the panel settles. On close, rows fade before the glass contracts and the trigger glyphs return as the capsule settles. Near the final close, a nearby row highlight briefly appears linked to the retracting glass while its label and chevron stay crisp. The recording does not establish what triggered that row effect. This is a **visual sequence**, not evidence of which framework API produced it or a timing rule to copy.

For a Commander control with this goal, first determine who owns the menu and its source geometry. A standard menu or popover may already animate from its trigger. A custom, same-container menu can use an entering glass effect with `.matchedGeometry` when the source is within container spacing, or a persistent panel whose shape and frame follow one animated progress value. Keep the source visually connected during the stretch and stage foreground readability separately. Do not change union IDs or substitute a material opacity fade for moving geometry. Only promise matched-glass behavior for effects that actually share a `GlassEffectContainer`; a system menu hosted elsewhere owns its own transition.

Commander's context meter is the worked example of the persistent path. [Lens morph](lens-morph.md) records a frame-by-frame reading of the iOS Mail "…" menu. The control's glass becomes a round lens that keeps growing while it travels, with the menu's rows magnified and blurred inside, then takes the menu's shape. It also records why an earlier drop-then-spread version with two springs was replaced by one value with staged windows, and how the same lens was shared with iOS.

Use the recording's ordering as a design reference:

| Phase | Material | Foreground and action meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compact rest | Settled trigger capsule | Trigger icons are crisp; destination rows are absent or inactive. |
| Opening stretch | A continuous source-to-destination stretch where the chosen path supports it | Retiring glyphs leave before they collide with incoming content. The trigger remains available for cancellation when the product permits it. |
| Destination becomes readable | Panel develops its usable content region | Labels and row icons become readable in that region; actions activate according to the control's explicit policy. |
| Open rest | Settled destination outline | Full menu interaction, keyboard order, labels, and dismissal behavior work. |
| Closing stretch | Destination contracts toward its source | Outgoing rows leave before their region becomes too small; focus returns to a suitable visible control. |
| Compact rest restored | Trigger capsule settles | Source glyphs are legible and no invisible destination consumes input. |

Optical softness in the recording is not an instruction to blur the entire SwiftUI subtree. Inspect the framework transition's own material/content treatment first. A manual blur, scale, general matched-geometry effect, and glass transition layered together can distort text twice and obscure which animation owns the result. Add only the treatment a demonstrated intermediate-frame problem requires. A persistent guest is different: no framework transition treats its content, so a lens that carries the destination's rows scales and blurs them itself, from the same progress value that moves the outline. That is one owner, not a second layer. The context meter does this and masks the rows to outside the host; see [lens morph](lens-morph.md#content-inside-the-lens).

## Plan what people see during overlap

These are Commander implementation criteria inferred from Apple's material behavior and its [guidance against crowded, overlapping controls](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technologyoverviews/adopting-liquid-glass#Controls). Apple does not prescribe milliseconds for icon fades during fusion.

| Foreground situation | Choreography |
| --- | --- |
| Two distinct actions remain in a roomy shared outline | Both icons may remain visible, as in Apple's two-symbol example. Keep each icon centered in its own legible region and each action's hit region unambiguous. The joined outline does not make the actions synonymous. |
| Two action frames collapse into the same lane or a guest becomes a small seed | Fade the outgoing guest symbol before the glyphs collide or exceed the guest's visible shape. On peel, reveal it once the growing shape has room, while the glass is still moving. Keep the material visible and clip foreground to the animated shape. |
| One persistent action changes its symbol | Use a content transition such as `.contentTransition(.symbolEffect(.replace))` within an animated transaction when replacement communicates a state change. It changes content **inside one view**; it does not synchronize symbols across separate fusing surfaces. A plain opacity fade is enough when the symbol simply hides or reveals. |
| A label or row of controls expands from a seed | Reveal readable text and enabled actions only after their region exists. Hide text before contraction makes it spill or cover another action. Derive visibility from the same reveal intent or progress so rapid reversal retargets the current pose. |

Let one geometry path move the material and give foreground opacity its own narrow animation scope. Choose reveal and hide points from the **visible space available to the glyph**, not a spring's nominal completion time. If staging uses delayed work, recheck the current destination before applying it; reversal must not flash a symbol from an abandoned direction. Keep the motion purposeful, brief, and interruptible, consistent with [Apple's motion guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion). Read [swiftui-animation-control](../../swiftui-animation-control/SKILL.md) when transaction scope or spring completion matters.

There are two useful implementation paths. A semantic visibility flag can retarget its own short opacity animation, with any staged work invalidated by revision/lifetime. When glyph fit must follow the shape continuously, an owned `Animatable` leaf can compute opacity from its interpolated progress along with dimensions. That interpolation must stay inside rendering; `@State` holding the target is not a presentation-progress source. [Content and accessibility](../../swiftui-animation-control/references/content-and-accessibility.md) explains the tradeoff, and [the persistent fixture](../examples/GlassMorphExamples.swift) demonstrates local progress mapping without per-frame model writes.

Opacity timing, action activation, and accessibility exposure are separate decisions. A glyph beginning to fade in does not prove its action has a usable target. Choose whether the action becomes available once its region exists or after settling, keep the reversal trigger available, and apply the same activation intent to keyboard/native focus and accessibility. Do not disable the entire control for a guessed spring duration to avoid handling interruption.

Before adding another guest to an existing Commander lane, map the other guests' resting and moving frames, draw order, and enabled hit regions. Container spacing considers all nearby effects, and a unified outline does not choose which overlapping action receives a click. Place or stage guests so that no two enabled actions claim the same point; verify combinations such as a jump chip, plan chip, and command panel being active together. [Commander's command-panel guide](../../../../docs/command-panel.md) records why shared-lane overlap needs a real interaction check.

[`ContentTransition.symbolEffect`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/contenttransition/symboleffect(_:options:)) is available for Commander's macOS 27 target; [`contentTransition(_:)`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/contenttransition(_:)) still needs an animation transaction. Apple's [SF Symbols guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sf-symbols) treats Replace as a way to convey a symbol's state change. Do not use it to imply that two different actions have become one.

Keep an icon or `Label` **inside** the SwiftUI view receiving `.glassEffect()` so the effect can apply its foreground legibility treatment. If a native `NSGlassEffectView` owns the material, put the icon or label in its `contentView`; a sibling glass view behind the content misses AppKit's intended foreground treatment. See [`NSGlassEffectView.contentView`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appkit/nsglasseffectview/contentview) and [Apple's macOS Liquid Glass video](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/310/). Check light/dark appearance and content scrolling underneath for contrast rather than assuming opacity alone guarantees legibility. A guest that has no glass at rest is the exception: see [a guest with no glass at rest](lens-morph.md#a-guest-with-no-glass-at-rest).

## Semantics and motion check

For a mounted but inactive guest, suppress clicks, focus, and accessibility exposure; give every visible icon an action label. A native representable needs its own accessibility and first-responder update as well as the SwiftUI wrapper. See [fusion rules](fusion-rules.md) for Commander's button hit shape and native-control handling. Apple's [adoption guide](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technologyoverviews/adopting-liquid-glass#Menus-and-toolbars) calls for accessible icon labels.

Inspect the fused rest pose, first separation, midpoint, and peeled rest pose in **both** directions. Reverse during the icon fade and during glass overlap. Check for duplicate or stray glyphs, clipped labels, ambiguous or dead click regions, and a stale delayed reveal after reversal. With Reduce Motion, snap Commander's geometry and keep the semantic destination clear; use a short cross-fade when a content replacement still needs explanation. On macOS, [`accessibilityPrefersCrossFadeTransitions`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/environmentvalues/accessibilitypreferscrossfadetransitions) follows Reduce Motion; it is not a separate Mac preference. Test reduced transparency and increased contrast in a running window, as Apple's [adoption guide](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technologyoverviews/adopting-liquid-glass#Visual-refresh) recommends for custom elements. Use [morph review](morph-review.md) for the diagnosis matrix and proportionate checks; endpoint screenshots and a build alone cannot establish morph behavior.
